01The brief · 00:32

One transformer, three regulated rails

Take power from the 230 V mains through a switch or MCB into a 50 Hz, laminated step-down transformer with three secondaries. Each secondary feeds its own chain, and each chain ends in a regulator.

  • Every chain: NTC thermistor → full bridge → capacitor → regulator → load
  • Rail 1: 15 V, discrete (series-pass Q1, op-amp, foldback limit)
  • Rail 2: 15 V, LM317 (adjustable IC regulator)
  • Rail 3: 5 V, 7805 (fixed IC regulator)
\underbrace{V_{\mathrm{C}}}_{\text{unregulated}} \;\longrightarrow\; \boxed{\text{regulator}} \;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{V_0}_{\text{steady}}

02The lab transformer · 02:14

Inside the laboratory transformer

EI laminations, a varnished wound coil and terminal lugs on top. The red and black lugs are the 0–230 V primary. There are three centre-tapped secondaries: 12-0-12, 15-0-15 and 9-0-9 V rms.

  • End to end gives double: 24, 30 or 18 V rms
  • End to centre gives the single value: 12, 15 or 9 V rms
  • The centre tap lets you build a centre-tapped rectifier; otherwise use the two ends
  • The windings are isolated from each other, so each rail gets its own ground
V_{\mathrm{peak}} = \sqrt{2}\,V_{\mathrm{rms}}V_{\mathrm{C}} \approx V_{\mathrm{peak}} - 2V_{\mathrm{D}} \qquad (V_{\mathrm{D}} \approx 0.7\,\mathrm{V})

03Rail 1 · 04:56

The discrete regulator, all together

Every block from this week in one schematic. The op-amp compares aV₀ with V_ref and drives Q1's base through R5 until the two are equal.

  • Pass element: Q1 carries the load current
  • Reference: R3 + zener, pot R4 taps off V_ref
  • Feedback: R1/R2 give aV₀; the op-amp is the error amplifier
  • Foldback limit: R_S senses I₀; Q2 with R_A/R_B steals Q1's drive
  • Op-amp protection: R5 limits the op-amp's output current
V_0 = \dfrac{V_{\mathrm{ref}}}{a}, \qquad a = \dfrac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}

04Q1's heat budget · 07:51

The unregulated window heats Q1

The 12-0-12 winding used end to end gives 24 V rms, about 34 V peak. With mains at 230 V ± 20 %, V_C swings from about 27 V to about 40 V, while the output stays at 15 V, 1 A (15 W).

  • V_CE = V_C − 15 V, from 12 V to 25 V
  • P_Q1 = V_CE · I₀, from 12 W to 25 W
  • The front end must supply 15 W + P_Q1, up to 40 W

Correction: the lecture says 35 V, 35 W and 50 W, but 40 − 15 = 25 V, which gives 25 W and 40 W. The lesson still holds: design for the high-mains corner.

P_{\mathrm{Q1}} = (V_{\mathrm{C}} - V_0)\,I_0 \qquad \eta = \dfrac{V_0}{V_{\mathrm{C}}}

05Heat sink design · 09:11

Sizing Q1's heat sink: a thermal circuit

T_J must stay below T_J,max = 150 °C. Heat flows through thermal resistances in series, the same way current flows through resistors: power acts like current, temperature like voltage.

  • θ_JC: junction → case (from the datasheet)
  • θ_CS: case → sink (insulating pad, thermal grease)
  • θ_SA: sink → air (the heat sink you buy)
  • On its own a package's θ_JA is tens of °C/W (LM317 TO-263 ≈ 38 °C/W). At these wattages you need a heat sink.
T_{\mathrm{J}} = T_{\mathrm{A}} + P_{\mathrm{D}}\,(\theta_{\mathrm{JC}} + \theta_{\mathrm{CS}} + \theta_{\mathrm{SA}})\theta_{\mathrm{SA}} \le \dfrac{T_{\mathrm{J,max}} - T_{\mathrm{A}}}{P_{\mathrm{D}}} - \theta_{\mathrm{JC}} - \theta_{\mathrm{CS}}

Try: load the 25 W worst case and find the largest θ_SA that keeps T_J under 150 °C.

06Op-amp protection · 10:22

R5: protecting the op-amp

An op-amp can source only 10–50 mA. The worst case is a shorted output: V₀ = 0, so Q1's base falls to I₀R_S + V_BE ≈ 1.2 V while Q2 sinks the drive. Almost the whole op-amp output voltage then sits across R5.

  • Draw at most 75 % of the rating: 50 mA → about 35 mA; 20 mA → 15 mA
  • In normal operation R5 only carries Q1's base current, I₀/β ≈ 10 mA
  • Size R5 for V₀ = 0, not for normal operation
R_5 \ge \dfrac{V_{\mathrm{OA}} - V_{\mathrm{B1}}}{0.75\,I_{\mathrm{rated}}} = \dfrac{(14 - 1.2)\,\mathrm{V}}{37.5\,\mathrm{mA}} \approx 340\,\Omega

Try: short the output, then shrink R5 until the op-amp overloads.

07Op-amp supply · 11:51

Powering the op-amp from the raw rail

V_C swings from 27 to 40 V, but the op-amp needs a steady supply. Tap the unregulated rail with a series resistor and a zener (a shunt regulator), designed for around 25–30 mA.

  • Size R_Z at low V_C and full load so the zener keeps ≥ 5 mA
  • Check high V_C and no load, when all of I_R flows in the zener: P_Z = V_Z · I_R
  • 15 V zener, R_Z = 820 Ω: I_R ≈ 15–30 mA, P_Z ≤ 0.46 W

Going further: to drive Q1's base to 15 + 0.5 + 0.6 ≈ 16.1 V, the op-amp's supply must sit a volt or two above that. The lecture's 15 V supply suits lower outputs. A 24 V zener works here, but it needs R_Z ≈ 200 Ω and dissipates more.

R_{\mathrm{Z}} = \dfrac{V_{\mathrm{C,min}} - V_{\mathrm{Z}}}{I_{\mathrm{op,max}} + I_{\mathrm{Z,min}}}

Try: sweep V_C from 27 to 40 V and watch I_Z absorb the change.

08Rail 2 · 12:51

LM317: three pins, adjustable output

The pins are IN, OUT and ADJ. The LM317 holds 1.25 V between OUT and ADJ, so a current of 1.25/R1 flows through R1. ADJ draws only about 50 µA, so the same current also flows through R2.

  • About 1.5 A, with internal current limiting and thermal shutdown
  • Needs V_in − V₀ > 3 V
  • P = (V_in − V₀) · I₀, so it needs a heat sink to keep T_J ≤ 150 °C
  • TI datasheet: R1 = 240 Ω is typical; θ_JA ≈ 38 °C/W (TO-263)
V_0 = 1.25\,\mathrm{V}\left(1 + \dfrac{R_2}{R_1}\right) + I_{\mathrm{ADJ}}\,R_2

Try: set 15 V, then lower V_in until the regulator drops out.

09IC regulators · 14:50

LM723: our circuit inside one chip

The LM723's internal schematic is the discrete regulator we built: a zener reference, an error amplifier, a series-pass transistor and a current-limit transistor. Its sense pins (CL, CS) are brought out, so you add your own R_S.

  • Inside the chip: reference, error amp, series pass, current limiter
  • You add: R_S, the output divider and, for more current, a boost transistor as a Darlington
  • The LM317 and 78xx go further: everything is inside, with just 3 pins

10Rail 3 · 19:24

7805 and the 3 V headroom rule

The 78xx fixed regulators (7805 = 5 V, 7812 = 12 V, 7815 = 15 V…) need no external parts: just input, output and common. They supply up to 1.5 A with protection built in.

  • Keep V_in − V₀ > 3 V, even at the bottom of the ripple and at low mains
  • For a 7805, V_C > 8 V under any condition, so choose the winding to suit
  • Full-bridge ripple: ΔV ≈ I₀ / (2fC)
  • Heat-sink the 7805, the LM317 and Q1 alike (T_J ≤ 150 °C)
  • TI lists about 2 V typical dropout at 1 A; the 3 V rule leaves a margin
V_{\mathrm{C,min}} = V_{\mathrm{peak}} - \Delta V \ge V_0 + 3\,\mathrm{V}\Delta V \approx \dfrac{I_0}{2fC}

11The experiment · 22:06

Testing: line and load regulation

For each rail, measure how V₀ changes. Line regulation: vary V_i with an autotransformer (variac) on the primary at a fixed load. Load regulation: vary R₀ to change I₀ at a fixed V_i.

  • Take about 5 readings for each test and tabulate them
  • Plot V₀ (y) against V_i (x), then V₀ (y) against I₀ (x); the independent variable goes on x
  • A good regulator gives flat lines. The edges show dropout (low V_i) and the current limit (high I₀).
  • Each rail here uses its own winding: 24, 18 or 9 V rms
\text{Line: } \dfrac{\Delta V_0}{\Delta V_{\mathrm{i}}} \qquad\qquad \text{Load: } \dfrac{\Delta V_0}{\Delta I_0}

Try: press a Sweep button, or move a slider and click Record.

QUESTION 1

A 15-0-15 winding used end to end. Roughly what peak voltage reaches the capacitor (ignore diode drops)?

42 V. End to end is 30 V rms, and 30 × √2 ≈ 42 V.

QUESTION 2

Mains is at +20 %. The discrete rail gives 15 V at 1 A from V_C = 40 V. What does Q1 dissipate?

25 W. (40 − 15) V × 1 A. 40 W is what the front end must supply in total.

QUESTION 3

When is the op-amp most at risk of over-current through R5?

Output shorted. V₀ = 0 drops Q1's base to about 1.2 V, so almost the whole op-amp voltage sits across R5.

QUESTION 4

LM317 with R1 = 240 Ω. Which R2 gives about 5 V?

720 Ω. 1.25 × (1 + 720/240) = 1.25 × 4 = 5 V.

QUESTION 5

A 7805's capacitor ripples between 7.4 V and 9 V. What happens?

It dips. The headroom rule has to hold at the valley, not on average.

QUESTION 6

In a load-regulation test, what is the independent variable (the x-axis)?

I₀. Vary R₀ to set I₀ and plot V₀ against it. For line regulation, V_i is on x.

✓Recap

What to take to the bench

1

One multi-winding transformer feeds three chains (NTC → bridge → C → regulator): a discrete 15 V rail, an LM317 at 15 V and a 7805 at 5 V.

2

At ±20 % mains, V_C runs from about 27 to 40 V, so Q1 sees 12–25 V and up to 25 W. Size its heat sink for the high-mains corner.

3

R5 protects the op-amp (worst case: a shorted output), and a resistor plus zener gives the op-amp a steady supply.

4

LM317: V₀ = 1.25 (1 + R2/R1). The LM723 is our discrete design inside one IC. The 78xx parts are fixed and need just three pins.

5

Every IC regulator needs more than 3 V of headroom at the ripple valley, plus a heat sink. Check each rail with V₀–V_i and V₀–I₀ plots.
